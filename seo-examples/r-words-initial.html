Tips from our SLPs

Why /r/ is trickyThe tongue has to pull back and bunch up or curl, without touching anything. It’s hard to see, which makes it hard to copy.
What you might hearYoung kids often swap in /w/: “wabbit” for rabbit, “wed” for red.
Make it a gameBe the listener: say a word right or wrong and let your child judge it. Hearing the difference is a big first step.

When do kids learn /r/?

/r/ is one of the last sounds children learn. Many kids say it clearly between ages 5 and 6 (Crowe & McLeod, 2020), so “wabbit” for “rabbit” is common in younger children.

Questions parents ask

At what age should a child say the /r/ sound?

/r/ comes late. Many children master it between ages 5 and 6, and some later. If your child is older and still working on /r/, an SLP can help.

Why does my child say “w” instead of “r”?

This is called gliding, and it’s common while /r/ is developing. If it continues past the typical age, talk with a speech-language pathologist.

Can an app help with /r/?

Practice at home helps most when it follows what your SLP is working on. SpeechLP lets SLPs assign the exact words and positions, and shows them how practice went.
