Why it matters

  • Home practice is standard but rarely measured (Sugden et al., 2016).
  • Dose is understudied. Frequency matters (Allen, 2013); how home trials and spacing relate to gains is open.
  • Automated scoring needs validation. Promising in Swedish, Korean and Mandarin (Getman et al., 2022; Kim et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2024), but not yet validated for English home practice.

First look: our preprint

In 101 returning profiles, app-scored accuracy on the same sounds rose from 69.4% to 74.9% across two consecutive 30-day periods (+5.5 pp, 95% CI 0.4 to 10.4), while practice sessions fell from 6.7 to 3.4 per child.

Descriptive and observational: no comparison group, and the app's scoring hasn't yet been checked against SLP ratings. That's the gap we want a partner to close. Silver et al., 2026, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23932485

Two starter studies

1. Scoring validationCould support a co-authored ASHA submission
Compare the app's correct/incorrect decisions with blinded ratings from several SLPs, acoustic measures and parent judgments, in children aged 3–12. An English replication of Getman et al. (2022). SpeechLP doesn't store audio, so the partner records study sessions under its own IRB.
2. Home-practice doseUses existing data
Do the amount, spacing and accuracy of home practice predict gains? How closely do families complete SLP-assigned practice? De-identified data under a data use agreement.
